Robot Acoustic

The world as a robot with 8 microphones on its head hears it. A man says “Hi there, how are you doing today?”, a woman answers “I'm good. What is the plan for the day?”, and a baby cries, while the robot's own fan, servos and electronics add noise right next to its microphones.

What this page is for: to hear and measure how well the robot can pick out one person's voice when other people, a baby and its own body are making noise. It simulates the robot's 8 microphones for the scene you set up, then runs the same processing as the Python scripts: direction finding (where each sound comes from) and beamforming (combining the 8 microphones to focus on one speaker). Use it to try ideas quickly, such as a louder fan, a closer speaker or a more echoey room, before running the full Python simulation.

How to use it

  1. Pick a sceneUnder Knobs, start with Baby crying, Baby + fan, Robot moving or Quiet room.
  2. Change the soundsSwitch robot noises on or off, set their loudness, move the baby or change the echo. Drag M, W and B around the head. The mics re-render in a few seconds.
  3. Listen to the raw micsPress Play selected mics. Click mics on the head to choose which you hear. Use Stereo with headphones.
  4. Compare the processingUnder Pick out each speaker, play Mic 1 alone, then the beamformers aimed at the man or the woman.
  5. Look at the soundClick any row to see its waveform and spectrogram. Click the picture to play from that moment.
  6. Read the scoresThe Scores table compares each method with the original (mic 1 alone) and with Python. Download any sound with ↓.

Scores

Each score is how much stronger the target voice is than everything else at that point, in dB. The original is Mic 1 alone: what one microphone hears with no processing. Gain is how much the processing improves on the original. +3 dB means the voice is about twice as strong relative to the noise, and +10 dB means ten times.

      Python recordings

      The WAV files made by sim_conversation.py and sim_8mic_robot_noise.py with pyroomacoustics. Open one to compare it with the page's version.

      How the page makes the sound

      Same room (6 × 5 × 3 m), 8-mic ring, voices, robot noise recipes, levels and beamformers as the Python scripts. The room is lighter: exact wall reflections up to 3 bounces plus a statistical late echo. Scores land within about 0.5 dB of Python in the baby and fan scenes.

      Footsteps are approximate

      The page's beamformer cancels the footstep thump much less than Python does, so footsteps start switched off. Switch them on to hear them, but don't read too much into the scores.

      Loudness levels

      Every level is loudness at mic 1 compared with the man's voice, as in the scripts' LEVEL_DB and BABY_DB.
