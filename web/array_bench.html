How the page computes this

          
    
            
  • Same geometry as Python: array at the room centre, 1.5 m high, mic 1 at 0°, angles counter-clockwise.
    • 
            
  • Sound reaching each mic = the direct path plus wall reflections up to 2 bounces (exact image sources), plus late echo as a diffuse field whose level comes from Sabine's formula.
    • 
            
  • Background noise is independent on every mic, set to the SNR at mic 1, as in Python.
    • 
            
  • Beamformers use the same far-field steering, delay-and-sum weights and MVDR (MPDR form, mixture covariance, 1e-3 loading) as sim_8mic_room.py.
    • 
            
  • dB scores cover 0–8 kHz and are weighted by the measured spectrum of the two speech clips. Talker A's clip is 2.5 dB louder than B's, as in Python. Direction finding uses 300 Hz up to the band-top knob.
    • 
          

        

        

          
How far to trust it

          
Use it to explore trends and choose scenes. Then confirm in Python. Checked against every experiment scene: delay-and-sum and mic-1 scores land within about 0.5 dB of summary.txt in most scenes, MVDR within about 1.5 dB, and SRP-PHAT within a few degrees. MUSIC is the least reliable match, because reflections beyond two bounces and the changing speech content are left out. Choose a preset to see the Python result for that scene under each tile.

          
Change any knob and the Python line disappears, since that scene has not been simulated yet.
